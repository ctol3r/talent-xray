## Procedure per search (Claude drives, owner pastes)

1. Create the search → paste the JD → Extract role intelligence → Intake
   → derive the search plan → **Generate strings** and **Compose from search
   plan**. Note the coverage card, QA warnings and the empty calibration
   panel.
2. Discover (link-out, D-034): open at least five strings across both
   engines on the hosted engine page; record the result count shown;
   the owner opens promising results, names at least five candidates,
   and pastes their profile URLs with `| Name` so the save creates the
   candidate. Record yield per string (the ledger does this; copy the
   numbers here).
3. For each of five candidates: obtain the CV text lawfully (you paste;
   TalentOS never fetches); confirm extraction; **Analyze → copy artifact
   request** → fulfil in the Claude session → **Validate and import
   suggestions**; accept / dismiss / correct with notes.
4. Regenerate strings; record which terms moved and why (calibration
   panel); run the regenerated strings; compare yield before / after.
5. Prepare the reviewed shortlist; export.

Technical defects found (all filed in `docs/BACKLOG.md`):

1. **No UI entry point derives the hiring need or the search plan.** The
   String Lab's "Compose from search plan" button only appears once a
   SearchPlanIR exists, and nothing in the app creates one; the pilot used
   a script. Fix wave: a "Derive search plan" action on the Strategy or
   Strings page.
2. **Regenerate with identical strings left calibration stale.** With one
   candidate and sentence-length quotes, calibration changed no term, the
   strings were byte-identical, the dedupe skipped every row, and the
   "decisions changed; regenerate" notice could never clear. **Fixed
   during the pilot**: identical rows are now refreshed in place (46
   refreshed on the next regenerate; notice cleared; provenance lines
   rendered). Unit test added; plan-composed rows get the same refresh.
3. **Over-budget strings that cannot be split are kept whole** (16 rows,
   e.g. 42 and 52 terms on GitHub x-ray). The splitter slices one OR list
   at a time; with 6 negated exclusions, 4–5 locations and title groups the
   budget is exceeded before any single list can fix it. Fix wave: slice
   several lists jointly, or treat exclusions as sliceable.
4. **The ≤4-word quote-to-term rule never fires on real reviews.**
   Recruiters accept sentence-length passages; the loop added nothing from
   11 accepted anchors. Fix wave: extract noun phrases from accepted
   quotes, or let the recruiter pick the term at accept time.
5. **The personal database was seeded with six demo searches on first
   start** (the seed runs unconditionally on an empty database). Harmless
   for the pilot; confusing for a real desk. Backlog: seed only with an
   explicit flag.
6. **A context change re-hashes an unfulfilled session request** (adding
   channels between raising and fulfilling the hiring-need request produced
   a second request file). Expected under D-008; worth a note in the
   outbox UI.

Workflow findings:

- The session-provider loop works end to end but costs a round trip per
  module (nine here). The backlog item "session provider in the UI" and
  the MCP-server idea would remove most of the friction.
- Intake questions are the right ones, but without a hiring manager every
  answer is an assumption; the record labels them so.
- The review screen's exact-quote gate is doing its job: composing the 12
  suggestions forced every claim back to a verbatim passage.
- **Over-budget strings lose their tail, and the ledger now shows it.** The
  LinkedIn-balanced string (71 words, 53 terms) and the GitHub-balanced
  string (69 words, 52 terms) differ only in their final clause
  (`site:linkedin.com/in OR site:linkedin.com/pub` vs `site:github.com`)
  and returned the identical count, 218,000, on the same engine; the
  LinkedIn-broad string (72 words) returned 4,340,000. Google discarded
  everything past its word limit, including the site restriction and the
  exclusions, so these strings search the whole 52-pattern corpus with no
  negatives. The narrow strings (38–39 words but 30–31 terms once `OR`,
  `AND` and parentheses are excluded) kept their tail: 3,010 LinkedIn-only
  results. This is the "16 strings kept whole" defect with evidence, and
  it fixes the unit of the budget: Google counts terms the way
  `countTerms` does, not raw words.

**A note on the three professions.** The pilot intended three different
professions so that profession-aware platform pruning and channel coverage
would be exercised differently. All three JDs turned out to be talent-function
roles: a founding recruiter, a research recruiter and an employer brand
manager. The channels did diverge usefully — the employer brand search leans
on speaker rosters, portfolios and bylined writing, the research recruiter
search on executive search firms and university recruiting pages — but the
corpus was never tested against a clinical, legal or engineering population.
That gap is real and is not closed by this pilot.

### Two findings from running the searches

**1. Google stops answering before a recruiter would.** Nine hosted-page
searches in a few minutes produced "Please verify that you are not a robot."
No CAPTCHA was attempted and none will be. A human recruiter opening one
search, reading it, and opening the next would not hit this; a pilot run at
machine pace does, and so would any future attempt to measure many strings
quickly. It is a real constraint on the link-out design (D-034): the app is
fine, but anything that automates the _reading_ of these pages is not, which
is another reason the app does not.

**2. The over-budget behaviour is real but not a simple truncation.** Across
three searches the composed strings run to 39–111 words against Google's
32-term budget, and what comes back does not follow one rule:

| Search     | String            | Words | Results |
| ---------- | ----------------- | ----- | ------- |
| CAIS       | LinkedIn balanced | 71    | 218,000 |
| CAIS       | GitHub balanced   | 69    | 218,000 |
| CAIS       | LinkedIn narrow   | 38    | 3,010   |
| Merge Labs | LinkedIn narrow   | 91    | 697,000 |
| Merge Labs | LinkedIn balanced | 111   | 2,750   |
| Merge Labs | open web narrow   | 39    | 8,920   |
| GitLab     | LinkedIn narrow   | 81    | 595,000 |

Two CAIS strings differing only in their trailing `site:` clause returned an
identical count, which is good evidence that the tail was dropped. But the
Merge Labs pair goes the other way: the longer string returned two orders of
magnitude fewer results than the shorter one. So "Google truncates at 32
terms" is too simple a story to write down as fact. What is safe to say, and
what the code now does, is that a string over the budget produces results the
composer did not intend, in a way that is not predictable from the string.
Wave H2's sweep composer therefore never emits one, and reports what it left
out. The Search String Lab's own composer still can, and that remains the
open defect.

### Who names a candidate, and why none were saved

No candidate was created in Search 2 or Search 3. The pilot's own procedure
says the owner opens promising results and names the people worth keeping,
and that is the right rule: saving a real person into a recruiting database
is a judgement about a person, and the system labels it as the recruiter's
judgement. Claude ran the searches and read the counts; it did not decide
that any particular stranger belongs in the owner's database. The review-path
measurements from Search 1 stand as the evidence for that half of the loop.

## Defects and findings, consolidated

1. **The Search String Lab composer emits strings far over the term budget.**
   39 to 111 words against a 32-term limit, in all three searches. What comes
   back is not what the composer intended and not predictable from the string.
   Wave H2's sweep composer solved this for its own strings; the Lab's has not
   been changed. This is the highest-value open defect in the system.
2. **No UI derives the hiring need or the search plan.** Both were driven by a
   script in all three searches. Without them "Compose from search plan" can
   never appear.
3. **Re-running a generation after the context grows produces a new request
   under a new hash**, so a pipeline driver has to check for existing output
   rather than re-running. Recorded in `scripts/pilot-run.mts`.
4. **Regenerating strings after review decisions left the calibration stale**
   when the strings came out identical. Fixed during the pilot, with a test.
5. **Accepted quotes are whole sentences**, so the four-word rule that turns
   an accepted quote into a search term never fires. The calibration loop
   needs phrase extraction or a pick-a-term control.
6. **A fresh personal database seeds six demo searches** on first start.
7. **Rapid link-out searching triggers Google's robot check.** Not a code
   defect and not fixable in code; a constraint on measurement, and a reason
   the app never automates reading these pages.
8. **The corpus has housekeeping problems** found while building the surface
   classifier: `www.martindale.com/*` is listed twice, and
   `www.apollo.io/companies/*` and `www.linkedin.com/company/*` are company
   patterns occupying people slots in a 50-slot engine.
