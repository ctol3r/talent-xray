# Where HSAL fits — answer for the owner (2026-09-07)

Question asked: _"where does HSAL fit into all of this?"_

Read for this answer: the HSAL repository (`/Users/christoler/hsal`),
`HSAL_INTEGRATION.md`, `TALENTOS_HSAL_EXECUTION_PLAN.md`, `src/lib/hsal/*`,
the Diagnosis page, `identity.ts`, `calibration.ts`, `fair-hiring.ts`,
`LEGAL.md`, and the live HSAL database through its own MCP server.

---

## 1. In plain words

HSAL is already inside TalentOS, on one page: **Diagnosis**. It is where the
app keeps _what the recruiter believed, what they saw, what they tried, and
what they changed their mind about_ — kept apart from the recruiting work
itself, in a form no model can quietly edit.

A search binds to a decision case. The pipeline counts become a state. The
recruiter types what they think is wrong and how sure they are; that
confidence is theirs and only they can change it. Candidate observations and
"the hiring manager said X" become evidence. TalentOS's rules write five
competing explanations, rank experiments, and the recruiter picks one, records
the result, and explicitly revises the belief. That loop already ran for the
seeded demo search: supply-constraint confidence went 76% to 31% after a blind
adjacent-profile review, and the revision is attributable and dated.

**What HSAL is not, and must not become:** a candidate database. It has no
person, candidate or contact table. Recruiting vocabulary reaches it only as
opaque tags. TalentOS keeps the facts; HSAL keeps the epistemic layer over
them by reference.

**The rule that governs any integration:** the profile-connections feature
must work fully with HSAL unpaired. HSAL adds provenance and belief-tracking
on top; it is never a dependency of a basic feature.

| --- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| (a) | **Identity beliefs** — "these profile URLs are one person"         | A belief per candidate whose confidence the recruiter owns; each saved URL as evidence; the matcher's strengths as _proposed_ relations; a Claude session's separate assessment | The right destination, blocked on three HSAL changes, one of them legal |
| (b) | **Calibration beliefs** — "this requirement is really a must-have" | A belief per requirement; each review decision as evidence; the Wave F checkpoint as the revision moment                                                                        | Waits for Wave F, which is unbuilt                                      |
| (c) | **The pilot record**                                               | The owner's beliefs about the product, with each measured cell of the pilot as evidence                                                                                         | Do now. One hour, no app code                                           |
| (d) | **The discovery decision**                                         | D-033/D-034 as a decision case; the provider assessment as evidence; the four options as interventions                                                                          | Do now. One hour, no app code                                           |

### Why (a) is the right destination

It is the only one a recruiter sees on every candidate, and it is what turns
ten profile links into something an aggregator cannot show. A hireEZ profile
shows N links after a hidden aggregation step you cannot inspect. This would
show, per link: how it arrived, who asserted it, the human who accepted it,
that human's stated confidence that the set is one person, every conflicting
record, and a model's opinion kept in its own box, unable to move the number.

The doctrine already matches on both sides. `identity.ts` says `same_urls` is
"the only thing strong enough to suggest one person; still never auto-merged"
and its result type carries no merge instruction by design. HSAL's invariant
is the same sentence in another vocabulary: a system may propose, only a human
accepts, and confidence belongs to the human. D-032's "one human click
confirms" is a relation review. D-031's "unverified until a human flips it" is
`proposed` versus `accepted`.

### Why it is blocked, and by what

Three changes are needed in HSAL before a single candidate URL goes in. The
third is not a nice-to-have.

- **T-10** — HSAL has no "propose a relation" route for an agent. Without it
  TalentOS cannot hand the matcher's output to HSAL at all.
- **T-11** — a human's accept/reject carried by TalentOS is refused by core
  today, because the calling actor is `agent:talentos` rather than the human.
  The existing T-4 pattern (human actor id in the request, validated by core)
  is the fix.
- **T-15 — HSAL is append-only and has no redaction or deletion path.**
  `LEGAL.md` §5 promises any person can have what we hold about them deleted,
  and `deleteCandidate` honours that today. The moment a candidate's URLs
  exist as HSAL evidence, deleting the candidate no longer deletes everything.
  **Either HSAL gains a deletion path wired to the candidate delete, or (a)
  never holds anyone's data but the owner's.** This is a precondition, not a
  backlog item.

### One thing to fix in HSAL first, unrelated to the plan

The live database holds two byte-identical captures of the same policy page
(2026-09-06 and 2026-09-07) with two separate relations, one accepted and one
still proposed. The content-hash dedupe did not collapse them. Worth finding

## 3. Recommended order

1. **(c) and (d) now.** Both run through the HSAL CLI in about an hour each,
   need no application code, and are the honest form of rule 9: a competitive
   claim recorded as a belief with a confidence and the evidence behind it,
   rather than a sentence in a document. They also dogfood HSAL the way B17
   does for VitalCV.
2. **(a) after T-10, T-11 and T-15.** Until the deletion path exists, run it
   against the owner's own record only.
3. **(b) after Wave F ships.** It is partly duplicative today, and D-014 fixed
   requirement authority to `assertedBy` and `contested` "and nothing more" —
   so a confidence on a requirement may live in HSAL only, never on the
   canonical document, or D-014 reopens.

## 4. Epistemic rules for (a), when it is built

- A URL the recruiter **opened** through the companion is `observed`: the page
  existed at that address and they were on it. A URL **pasted or imported** is
  `user_asserted`: we know the recruiter asserts it, not that they looked.
  Neither is evidence that it is the same person; that is the belief the
  evidence supports.
- A **generated guess is never evidence.** A constructed URL is a hypothesis;
  it enters HSAL only after a human opens it and keeps it, and then as the
  ordinary kept-URL evidence.
- A **proposed relation never renders as accepted.** The card must show
  "proposed by TalentOS" and "accepted by you on `<date>`" as different
  states, and the word "verified" appears in neither.
- The **model may** assess a belief and, once T-10 exists, propose relations
  under its own actor. It may **never** change a confidence, review a
  relation, create a source row, fetch a URL, or merge records. The
  `claude-desktop` preset must never hold `relation:review` or any
  `belief:write` scope.
- The matcher's own output is `inferred`, never `observed`, and a `same_urls`
  hit between two records with different names proposes `contradicts` on both
  beliefs, not a merge.

## 5. What must never go into HSAL

- **No protected characteristics.** Every statement, reason and evidence
  content passes `scanTextForProtectedTraits` before the write; a hit blocks
  visibly. Extend the fair-hiring grep to `packages/hsal-adapter/src`.
- **No contact data.** Emails and phones stay in `candidate_source_evidence`;
  the NPPES office telephone and addresses stay in the registry table. Only
  the identifier and the match date travel.
- **No page content.** No snippets, page text, CV text, quotes or screenshots.
  Evidence content is the URL. A LinkedIn tab title encodes name, headline and
  employer, so titles follow the same rule the companion already applies.
- **No names by default.** Ids in statements; TalentOS renders names.
- **Do not use the HSAL Chrome extension on profile pages.** It sends
  highlighted page text as evidence content, which on a LinkedIn or GitHub
  page is profile content and breaks product rule 1. The TalentOS companion
  (URL and label only) is the capture path. If the two extensions ever merge,
  HSAL needs an "add this page as a source, URL only" action that reads no
  selection.
